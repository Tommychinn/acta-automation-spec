Part One

Structural cleanup

Two pieces of structure are wrong and should be corrected before the rules in Part Two are built on top of them.

1.1 Remove the onboarding pipeline

ProblemBooking through the ACTA Onboarding Meeting calendar lXE04vrbONLxGRFiIs07 creates an opportunity in a separate onboarding pipeline. Prospects should never enter that pipeline.
DoDelete the onboarding pipeline. Remove any workflow that creates opportunities in it.
ThenA booking on the ACTA Onboarding Meeting calendar lXE04vrbONLxGRFiIs07 moves the opportunity to Call Completed - Follow Up Booked in the Player Evaluation Meeting pipeline — the same outcome as booking through the Follow Up - ACTA Player Evaluation Meeting calendar hHD2MGGb5jMwouPNxEE4.
NoteThe ACTA Onboarding Meeting calendar lXE04vrbONLxGRFiIs07 itself stays live and in use. It is the onboarding pipeline and its opportunity-creating workflows that go.

1.2 Merge the duplicate follow-up stage

ProblemIn the PDS Pipeline, Follow Up - Player Evaluation Call Booked duplicates Qualified - Player Evaluation Booked. It has no counterpart in the Confirmation & Qualification pipeline, so the two pipelines cannot mirror each other cleanly.
Do, in order 1. Move every opportunity currently in Follow Up - Player Evaluation Call Booked into Qualified - Player Evaluation Booked.
2. Repoint every workflow that writes to the follow-up stage so it writes to Qualified - Player Evaluation Booked instead.
3. Delete Follow Up - Player Evaluation Call Booked.
Order mattersRepoint the workflows before deleting the stage, or a live automation will fail mid-flight.
Part Two

Booking, rebooking and rescheduling

The requirement in one line: a booking automation must fire on a genuine new booking and must not fire again when the same person reschedules or moves stage.

This is broken today — it is the problem you are being brought in to fix

What is happening now, and is wrong: pre-call engagement email sequences are firing on reschedules and on stage changes. They should fire on a genuine new booking and on nothing else. A prospect who simply moves an existing appointment is currently misjudged as a brand-new booking and is sent the new-booking sequence a second time. That is a live fault hitting real prospects. It is not the intended design and no part of it should be preserved.

Something in the account is causing that misjudgement, and finding it is part of the job. One or more automations are treating an existing prospect as new and triggering the new-booking workflow. ACTA's suspicion — unconfirmed, so verify rather than assume — is that the pipeline-syncing automations are implicated: a sync writes a stage change, and that stage change can look like a fresh booking event to a downstream trigger. Work through everything in New Sales Architecture 2026, establish what is actually firing and why, and switch off whatever is producing the fault.

You are not obliged to preserve any of it. Turning the whole folder off and rebuilding against this spec is an acceptable outcome if that is the faster route to correct behaviour.

The question is "has this person got, or ever had, a call booked with us" — expressed concretely as an existing opportunity in the PDS Pipeline. It is not about whether the appointment record is new.

Whether the two sequences sit in one automation with a branch or in two separate automations is the specialist's call. What is not optional is that the paths are distinct and driven by different triggers.

Part Three

Keeping the pipelines in sync

One prospect exists as an opportunity in more than one pipeline, so a stage change in one has to propagate. These rules come from ACTA's existing sync spec, checked here against the live stage lists.

The governing principle — read this before the tables

1. A stage that exists in only one pipeline has no sync bearing. Landing in it causes no stage change anywhere else. That covers every stage unique to PDS Confirmation & Qualification, and every stage unique to the Player Evaluation Meeting pipeline.

2. A mirrored move is unconditional — it does not depend on where the opportunity currently sits. When a sync moves an opportunity to a stage, it goes there from whatever stage it is in. Worked example: a Player Evaluation Meeting is booked, so the PDS Pipeline moves to Qualified - Player Evaluation Booked. The PDS Confirmation & Qualification opportunity must move to its matching Qualified - Player Evaluation Booked regardless of its current stage — including straight out of NC within 24 hours, Contacted - NC, Confirmed or Qualified. Do not build preconditions or ordering assumptions into the sync; there is no required path through the stages.

Derived from that principle — worth a sanity check with ACTA

Four live stages were never given an explicit sync rule. Applying principle 1 above: Rescheduled, Call Completed - Not Closed and Call Completed - Follow Up Booked exist only in the Player Evaluation Meeting pipeline, so they propagate nowhere. Rescheduled PDS Appt exists in both PDS pipelines, so it syncs bidirectionally between them like the other shared PDS stages, and does not touch PEM. 3.4 Safeguards

  1. No infinite loops. A stage update caused by a sync workflow must not trigger an equivalent update back the other way.
  2. No duplicate writes. Do not write a stage value if the opportunity is already in that stage.
  3. The booking event is the source of truth for PEM and follow-up bookings. Manual pipeline edits do not drive the automation.
Part Four

An architecture question for you

The automations live in an automations folder called New Sales Architecture 2026, containing two subfolders: PDS Pipeline and Player Evaluation Pipeline. Known workflows include:

  • PDS Appointment Booked + Rescheduled — carries much of the pre-call engagement content
  • Player Evaluation Appointment Booked + Reschedule — the PEM equivalent
  • Player Evaluation Follow Up Appointment Booked

Alongside these sit roughly thirty separate workflows whose only job is syncing stages between pipelines — one per combination.

What ACTA wants from you here

Thirty workflows to maintain one behaviour looks like the wrong shape, but that is an outside view and it may be the right call in GoHighLevel. Review it and say plainly whether a smaller, more maintainable structure achieves the same result — and if so, what it looks like. If thirty is genuinely the correct approach in this platform, say that too, and it stays.

Two things worth weighing. Custom code is on the table: ACTA has previously built pipeline syncing by hand in code rather than as workflows, and that may well be the cleaner route here — a single implementation of one rule, instead of thirty copies of it drifting apart. And the sync workflows are a suspect in the misfiring described in Part Two, so whatever replaces them should be built so that a sync-driven stage change cannot be mistaken for a new booking downstream.

Outstanding

1. One inferred sync rule. The four stages that had no explicit rule are resolved in Part Three by applying ACTA's stated principle, not by separate instruction. The one worth a second look is Rescheduled PDS Appt, which this spec syncs bidirectionally across the two PDS pipelines because it exists in both.

2. Sequence names. This spec defines which sequence fires and when. The four sequences — new-booking PDS, returning PDS, PEM first-booking, PEM rebooking — exist in the CRM but have not been named here. Match them by function when building.

3. The copy itself is believed correct and already written. Part of the job is confirming that the right existing copy is attached to the right trigger, rather than writing anything new.
