Payments Integration Spec

Record every payment from Whop, Commas (formerly Fanbasis) and Kajabi, plus manually entered ones, against the right contact in the CRM. That covers all history now and every new transaction from now on. The result is one payment ledger per customer that everyone trusts, and an opportunity value that is exact to the cent.

Prepared for ACTA Football Version 2.4 Current state read from the live CRM, 2 Oct 2026

The one rule everything serves

Non-negotiable

The sum of a customer's ledger = their Lifetime Value = the value of their opportunity. All three are always the same number, to the cent, and all three match what the payment processors and manual entries show. Every phase below ends with proof of this.

Anything in this typeface is a literal value in the CRM. All amounts are US dollars.

Context

What exists today

Old payment fields

All six old fields are replaced by the new Payments folder. Whatever writes to them is switched off at go-live (Phase 5). Their data is not touched until the very last step, after it has been checked against the new ledger (Phase 7).

Opportunity values

  • Customers are marked Won in several pipelines at once, and some carry the full deal value in each. One customer shows $5,997 in three pipelines, so they count as $17,991 in any total.
  • 303 open opportunities in PDS AI SDR Lead Pipeline P&O carry a placeholder $3,000. That is not real money.

Payment links

On the call: the standard links stay. Payment links are created manually, one per product, and kept in a list. Reps pick the right one and share it in the chat during the sales call. ACTA prioritises conversion on the call, so this doesn't change. The rep tells the prospect to pay using the same email they booked the call with.

After the call: a personal link (new). For a prospect who will pay later, outside the call, the rep can trigger a personal link for a chosen product, which is emailed to the prospect. That link carries the prospect's CRM contact ID as hidden checkout data, so the payment matches to them exactly, whatever email they pay with. It uses the same product and plan as the standard link, so price, checkout and product reporting are unchanged. Whop and Commas both support this. Confirm for Kajabi.

Part one

What to build

A Payments folder on the contact

One contact-field folder called Payments holds everything below. Nothing payment-related lives outside it.

The ledger

Create a custom object called Payment, associated with the contact. Each transaction is one record, like a row in a table, not a separate custom field. The contact page lists the records one after another, with an identical structure whether a customer has 1 payment or 50. Records can be created by the integration or by a rep by hand (Rule 6). If ACTA's plan doesn't support custom objects, raise it at Checkpoint 1.

Part two

Rules

1 · Ledger = Lifetime Value = opportunity valueThe ledger mirrors the processors' transaction flow exactly: every payment, failed attempt, refund and chargeback as each processor records it. Lifetime Value is the sum of its succeeded records, and the opportunity value is set to the same number. If the three ever differ, that is a defect.
2 · Which opportunityThe value goes on exactly one opportunity per customer: the Player Evaluation Meeting opportunity if one exists, otherwise the PDS Pipeline opportunity (customers closed on the first call). Every other opportunity for that customer, including PDS Confirmation & Qualification, is set to $0. The 303 placeholder $3,000 values are cleared to $0.
2b · Stage on a closed dealA successful payment for a core programme (ACTA One, ACTA Elite or ACTA Pro) moves that same opportunity to Won. Other products do not move stages.
3 · One unit, no duplicatesThe processors report amounts differently. Whop and Commas use dollars, Kajabi uses cents, and Commas sends some prices as text. Convert everything to dollars on the way in. Processing the same transaction twice, from a re-sent event or a backfill re-run, must change nothing.
4 · Matching a payment to a contactIn order: (a) the CRM contact ID carried by a personal link; (b) an exact email match, including additional emails; (c) an exact phone match; (d) the processor customer ID already linked to a contact. The first step that finds exactly one contact wins. Never match on name alone. Anything else goes to reconciliation (Phase 3) and stays out of every total until it's resolved. After go-live, an unmatched payment alerts the rep who owns the deal the same day, so they can confirm who paid while the call is fresh.
5 · Second emails and mergesWhen a customer is confirmed to have paid with a second email, add it to their contact as an additional email so future payments match automatically. Merge two contacts only when Tommy approves. The surviving contact keeps every ledger record from both, and its totals are recalculated.
6 · Manual paymentsFor bank transfers, cash and anything else that never passed through a processor, a rep adds a Payment record by hand. Date, amount, product, method and reference are all required. It counts exactly like any other payment. Never enter manually a payment that went through a processor, because it would be counted twice.
7 · Nothing silentA transaction that fails to write is retried, then logged and alerted. Commas does not resend missed events and Kajabi sends no refund or failure events at all, so the daily check (Phase 6) is the safety net, not an extra.
Part three

Process

At each checkpoint, send Tommy the listed deliverables in writing, then stop until he approves.

Phase 0 · Access and backup

  1. Tommy provides API access to Whop, Commas and Kajabi. Ask for exactly what you need.
  2. Export every contact and every opportunity, with all fields, before changing anything.

Phase 1 · Discovery (no changes)

  1. What writes the old fields. Find what fills Revenue, Payment Date and the purchased-… tags (believed to be a Zapier zap). Check the sub-account's audit logs. List everything that relies on it. The purchased-… tags must keep being applied after switch-off: replace that job, don't lose it.
  2. Payment links. Confirm the current list of standard links: which product and processor each belongs to. Map them to the product categories. Propose how a rep triggers a personal link (e.g. pick the product on the contact, then send) and what the email says.
  3. Field mapping. For each processor, show how its data maps to every ledger field, and flag any gaps. Known gaps to plan around: Kajabi gives no failed payments, no refund or dispute events, and no card details; Commas' history has no date filter and no payment method; only Whop gives card last 4 on every payment. Confirm whether Kajabi takes payments through Kajabi Payments or ACTA's own Stripe or PayPal, because failure and card detail may have to come from there.
  4. Payer details. Confirm whether each processor gives name, email and phone on every payment. Where one doesn't, say how missing details will be collected when a new contact is created.
  5. Product mapping. List every product name on every processor with its proposed category.
  6. Your technical approach: where the logic runs, how retries and alerts work, any running cost, and confirmation that custom objects are available.
Checkpoint 1

Send items 1 to 6. Tommy approves the mapping, the approach and who receives alerts.

Phase 2 · Build the structure

  1. Create the Payments folder, its fields and the Payment custom object as specified and approved.

Phase 3 · Backfill dry run and reconciliation (nothing written to contacts yet)

  1. Pull every historical transaction from all three processors and match each one using Rule 4.
  2. Per processor, report the transaction count and the totals for payments, refunds and chargebacks. These must equal the processor's own dashboard totals exactly.
  3. Build the reconciliation sheet with one row per payer (not per payment), so one decision covers all of that person's payments. Show: the payer's name, email and phone as the processor shows them; their payments (dates, amounts, products); the proposed CRM contact (name, email, link) or "no match found"; and how the match was made. Rows matched by personal-link contact ID or exact email are pre-filled as confirmed, for a quick scan. All other rows are left blank for Tommy.
  4. For each row, Tommy picks one outcome:
    • Yes: this is the right contact.
    • No, it's this contact instead: Tommy gives the right one.
    • No contact exists: create one from the payer's details, adding any that are missing.
    A spreadsheet is fine. If you have a better interface for making these decisions quickly, propose it.
  5. Give the resulting Lifetime Value for every customer.
Checkpoint 2

Send the dry-run totals and the reconciliation sheet. Tommy completes the sheet. Nothing is written until every row has an outcome.

Phase 4 · Backfill for real

  1. Apply the reconciliation outcomes: create the new contacts, add the second emails and make the approved merges.
  2. Write the ledger, calculate every Payments field, and set opportunity values under Rule 2, including clearing duplicates and placeholders.
  3. Run it again. Nothing should change (Rule 3).
Checkpoint 3

Show three totals side by side, which must match exactly: the processors' totals, the dry-run totals, and the CRM ledger totals. Then Tommy picks 10 customers and you show each one's ledger next to their processor records.

Phase 5 · Go live

  1. Switch on live updates from all three processors, and switch off whatever was writing the old fields (making sure the purchased-… tags still get applied).
  2. Switch on personal links. Test one end to end: the rep triggers it, the prospect receives the email, pays with a different email, and the payment still lands on the right contact.
  3. Test each processor: a payment, a failed payment and a refund (and a chargeback, if there's a test mode). Then test one manual payment and one manual refund. Show that the ledger, the Payments fields and the opportunity value all update within 5 minutes, and that a repeated event changes nothing.
Checkpoint 4

Send the test results.

Phase 6 · Keep it honest

  1. Set up a daily automatic check that pulls the previous day's transactions from each processor and compares them with the ledger. It adds anything missing, such as a missed event or a Kajabi refund, and alerts on any difference it can't resolve. The same daily report lists every new manual entry (who, amount, reference) for a manager to verify against the bank.
  2. Write a one-page guide for reps: tell every prospect to pay with the email they booked with; how to resolve an unmatched-payment alert; how to record a manual payment, and when not to.
Checkpoint 5

Send the first three daily check results, the rep guide and the location of the failure log.

Phase 7 · Check the old data, then delete it (final step)

  1. Compare old against new. For every contact with data in the old Revenue or Payment Date fields, compare it with their new ledger, after converting the old values' mixed cents and dollars.
    • The ledger shows more than the old fields: expected and fine. The new setup captures more.
    • The old fields show a payment the ledger doesn't have: a probable missed payment. Investigate each one, find it in the processors or confirm it's an error in the old data, and list it.
  2. Once Tommy has approved every gap, clear the data from the six old fields, confirm no workflow uses them, then delete the fields themselves.
Final report

Send the old-vs-new comparison (contacts checked, where the ledger has more, every gap and how it was resolved) before deleting anything. Then confirm the fields are deleted.

Scope

Done means

  • Every payment, failed attempt, refund and chargeback since ACTA started selling sits in the ledger exactly once, against the right contact.
  • For every customer, ledger sum = Lifetime Value = opportunity value, on exactly one opportunity, matching the processors to the cent.
  • New transactions appear within 5 minutes, the daily check catches anything missed, and reps can record off-processor payments.
  • The old payment fields and placeholder opportunity values are gone, and the purchased-… tags still work.
  • A core programme payment moves the deal to Won.
  • Reps can email a personal payment link for off-call closes, and those payments match exactly.
  • Out of scope: changing the standard links used on calls.
