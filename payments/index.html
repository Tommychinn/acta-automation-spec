Payments Integration Spec

Record every successful payment, refund and chargeback from Whop, Commas and Kajabi against the right contact in the CRM. That covers all history now and every new transaction from now on. The result is one payment record per customer that everyone trusts, and an opportunity value that is exact to the cent.

Prepared for ACTA Football Version 1.0 Current state read from the live CRM, 2 Oct 2026

The standard

Non-negotiable

For every customer, Lifetime Value in the CRM must equal what the three payment platforms say that customer has paid, net of refunds and chargebacks, to the cent. The opportunity value must equal Lifetime Value. Close is not good enough. Every phase below ends with proof of this.

Anything in this typeface is a literal value in the CRM. All amounts are US dollars.

Part one

What to build

A Payments folder on the contact

Create one contact-field folder called Payments holding everything below. Nothing payment-related lives outside it.

FieldTypeHolds
Lifetime ValueMonetaryThe total of all successful payments, minus refunds and chargebacks, plus any chargebacks later won back. This is cash actually received. It is calculated from the ledger only and is never typed in.
Revenue BookedMonetaryThe full agreed price of everything the customer has bought, including instalments not yet paid. Taken from the product catalogue (see below).
Total RefundedMonetaryRefunds plus chargebacks, as a positive number.
First Payment DateDateThe date of the earliest successful payment.
Last Payment DateDateThe date of the most recent successful payment.
Products PurchasedMulti-selectEach product bought, using the catalogue names.
Payment Review NeededCheckboxTicked when a payment couldn't be matched to this contact with certainty (see Rule 6).

The payment ledger

Create a custom object called Payment, associated with the contact, with one record per transaction. This is the single source of truth: every field above is calculated from it. If ACTA's plan doesn't support custom objects, raise it at Checkpoint 1. Don't fall back to a text field without approval.

Ledger fieldHolds
Transaction IDThe platform's own ID. Must be unique, so the same transaction can never be recorded twice.
PlatformWhop, Commas or Kajabi.
TypePayment, Refund, Chargeback or Chargeback Reversal.
AmountUS dollars to 2 decimals. Positive for a payment or reversal, negative for a refund or chargeback.
DateWhen the platform recorded the transaction.
ProductThe catalogue name.
InstalmentFor example "2 of 3". Blank for one-off payments.
Related TransactionFor refunds, chargebacks and reversals: the ID of the original payment.
Payer EmailThe email used at checkout, kept for matching and audit.

Product catalogue

A table mapping every product on every platform to one ACTA product name, its full price and its instalment structure. It drives Products Purchased and Revenue Booked. You build it in Phase 1, and Tommy approves it.

Part two

Rules

1 · Only successful money countsRecord successful payments, refunds, chargebacks and chargeback reversals. Failed, pending and abandoned payments are not recorded.
2 · Gross amounts in dollarsRecord what the customer actually paid, before platform fees. Check each platform's units: some report cents. That mistake is already visible in today's Revenue field.
3 · Refunds and chargebacksA refund is a negative entry for the amount actually refunded, so partial refunds count at the partial amount, linked to the original payment. A chargeback is a negative entry when the money is taken back. If ACTA wins the dispute, a positive Chargeback Reversal entry follows.
4 · No duplicates, everProcessing the same transaction twice, from a re-sent event or a backfill re-run, must change nothing. The Transaction ID guarantees this.
5 · Opportunity valueEach customer's Lifetime Value appears on exactly one opportunity: the Player Evaluation Meeting opportunity if one exists, otherwise the PDS Pipeline opportunity (customers closed on the first call). Residency purchases count towards the same Lifetime Value. All other opportunities for that customer, including PDS Confirmation & Qualification, are set to $0. The 303 placeholder $3,000 values are cleared to $0. Whenever Lifetime Value changes, the opportunity value updates with it.
6 · Matching a payment to a contact, in order(a) The CRM contact ID passed through by the customer's payment link. (b) An exact email match, including the contact's additional emails. (c) An exact phone match, after normalising the format. (d) The platform's customer ID, if it's already linked to a contact. The first step that finds exactly one contact wins. Never match on name alone.
7 · When matching is uncertainIf no contact matches, create one and tick Payment Review Needed. If two different contacts match (e.g. the email points to one contact and the phone to another), record the payment against neither: put it on a review list for Tommy, keep it out of every total, and alert. When a customer is confirmed to have paid with a second email, add that email to their contact as an additional email so future payments match automatically.
8 · Merging contactsMerge only on approved evidence: a shared email, phone, payment-link contact ID or platform customer ID. The surviving contact keeps every ledger record from both, and its totals are recalculated immediately after the merge.
9 · Nothing silentAny transaction that fails to write is retried automatically. If it still fails, it is logged and someone is alerted. A payment must never simply go missing.
Part three

Process

At each checkpoint, send Tommy the listed deliverables in writing, then stop until he approves.

Phase 0 · Access and backup

  1. Tommy provides API access to Whop, Commas and Kajabi. Ask for exactly what you need. Read-only is enough until Phase 5.
  2. Export every contact and every opportunity, with all fields, before changing anything.

Phase 1 · Discovery (no changes)

  1. Find what writes Revenue and Payment Date today: the workflow, integration or app, and which platform feeds it.
  2. Payment links: do per-customer links exist, on which platforms, and does the CRM contact ID reach the platform's transaction record? Give the share of historical transactions that carry it.
  3. For each platform: how you'll pull the full transaction history; whether it sends live events for payments, refunds and chargebacks; and what units it reports amounts in.
  4. Product catalogue: every product, with price, instalment structure and your proposed ACTA product name.
  5. Your technical approach: where the logic runs (for example platform events into an automation tool, or custom code), how retries and failure alerts work, any running cost, and confirmation that custom objects are available.
Checkpoint 1

Send items 1 to 5. Tommy approves the catalogue, the approach and who receives alerts.

Phase 2 · Build the structure

  1. Create the Payments folder, its fields and the Payment custom object exactly as specified.

Phase 3 · Backfill dry run (nothing written to contacts yet)

  1. Pull every historical transaction from all three platforms and match each one using Rule 6.
  2. Report, per platform: number of transactions and totals for payments, refunds and chargebacks. These must equal the platform's own dashboard totals exactly.
  3. Report the matching results: count per match method, the list of unmatched transactions, the list of conflicts, and any proposed merges with the evidence for each.
  4. Give the proposed Lifetime Value for every customer.
Checkpoint 2

Send the dry-run report. Tommy resolves the conflicts and approves the merges.

Phase 4 · Backfill for real

  1. Make the approved merges.
  2. Write the ledger records, calculate every Payments field, and set opportunity values under Rule 5, including clearing duplicates and placeholders.
  3. Run it again. Nothing should change (Rule 4).
Checkpoint 3

Show three totals side by side, which must match exactly: the platforms' totals, the dry-run totals, and the CRM ledger totals. Then Tommy picks 10 customers and you show each one's CRM ledger next to their platform records.

Phase 5 · Go live

  1. Switch on live events from all three platforms, and switch off whatever was writing to Revenue and Payment Date.
  2. On each platform, test a payment and a refund (and a chargeback, if the platform has a test mode). Show that the ledger record, the Payments fields and the opportunity value all update correctly within 5 minutes, and that sending the same event twice changes nothing.
Checkpoint 4

Send the test results.

Phase 6 · Clean up and keep it honest

  1. Confirm no workflow uses the six old payment fields, then delete them.
  2. Set up a daily automatic check comparing each platform's totals with the CRM ledger for the previous day. Any difference triggers an alert to the approved recipient.
Final report

Send the deleted fields, the first three daily reconciliation results, and the location of the failure log.

Scope

Done means

  • Every successful payment, refund and chargeback since ACTA started selling sits in the ledger exactly once, against the right contact.
  • For every customer, Lifetime Value and the opportunity value match the platforms to the cent, on exactly one opportunity.
  • New transactions appear within 5 minutes, failures are retried and alerted, and the daily check is running.
  • The old payment fields and the placeholder opportunity values are gone.
